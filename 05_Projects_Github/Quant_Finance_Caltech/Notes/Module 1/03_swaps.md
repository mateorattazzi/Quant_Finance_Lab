# Swaps

## Definition

A swap is a contract in which two parties exchange:

- two series of future payments
- at predetermined times

## Key Idea

A swap is essentially a sequence of forward-type contracts.

## Interest Rate Swap

The most common type of swap.

One party pays fixed interest payments.

The other party pays floating interest payments.

## Floating Interest Rate

A floating rate is usually linked to a market rate, such as LIBOR.

LIBOR means London Inter-bank Offered Rate.

It represents a short-term rate at which banks lend to each other.

## Why Use an Interest Rate Swap?

The main purpose is to transform floating payments into fixed payments, or vice versa.

Example:

A company has a loan with a variable interest rate but wants predictable payments.

The company can enter a swap and exchange floating payments for fixed payments.

## Why Do Swaps Exist?

In efficient markets, fixed and floating payments should have equivalent value.

However, swaps exist because of:

- market imperfections
- different borrowing costs
- different regulations
- different credit quality
- different access to financial markets

## Regulatory Motivation Example

A pension fund may be required to increase exposure to fixed-income assets.

Instead of selling stocks, it can:

- keep the stocks
- enter a swap
- exchange stock returns for bond-like returns

This creates synthetic fixed-income exposure.

## Swaption

A swaption is an option to enter a swap.

Unlike a regular swap, a swaption is a non-linear derivative.

## Comparative Advantage in Swaps

Two firms may have different borrowing costs in different markets.

Even if one firm has higher borrowing costs everywhere, relative advantages may differ.

This can create swap opportunities.

## Role of the Investment Bank

An investment bank can act as intermediary.

It structures the swap so that the gain is shared between:

- Firm A
- Firm B
- the bank

## Important Insight

Apparent swap gains may disappear after considering:

- default risk
- credit risk
- market efficiency

## Equity Swaps

An equity swap exchanges stock returns for another type of return.

Example:

stock returns ↔ index returns

Purpose:

- diversification
- reduce concentration risk
- reshape portfolio exposure

## Example: Charitable Foundation

A foundation receives stock but cannot sell it.

It may enter an equity swap to:

- keep the stock legally
- exchange its returns for diversified index returns

## Example: Executive Compensation

Executives often receive company stock.

This creates concentrated portfolio risk.

If allowed, they could use a swap to exchange company stock returns for index returns.

## Important Quant Finance Ideas

Swaps are linear derivatives.

Like forwards, their payoff changes linearly with the underlying variables.

Unlike options, they do not create non-linear payoffs.

## Core Intuition

A swap changes the economic exposure of an asset without necessarily changing ownership of the asset.
