# Stocks, Bonds and Derivatives

## Financial Instruments

Financial markets trade two broad categories:

- basic securities
- derivatives / contracts

## Basic Securities

The two main basic securities are:

- stocks
- bonds

## Stocks

A stock represents partial ownership of a company.

Companies issue stocks to raise capital for:

- investments
- expansion
- research and development

## Stock Characteristics

Known today:

- current stock price

Unknown:

- future stock price

Stocks may also pay dividends.

## Bonds

A bond is essentially a loan to a company or government.

The issuer promises future payments to the bondholder.

## Bond Characteristics

Known:

- maturity payoff
- coupon payments
- payment dates

Unknown:

- market price before maturity

## Bond Terminology

## Face Value / Principal / Nominal Value

The final payment received at maturity.

Example:

$100 at maturity.

## Coupons

Intermediate fixed payments before maturity.

## Bond Risk

## Default-Free Bonds

Bonds with no assumed default risk.

Example:

US Treasury bonds.

## Credit / Default Risk

Risk that the issuer cannot make promised payments.

Examples:

- company bankruptcy
- sovereign default

## Derivatives

A derivative is a financial contract whose value depends on another asset.

The asset is called the underlying.

Examples of underlyings:

- stocks
- bonds
- interest rates
- currencies
- commodities

## Important Clarification

In finance, derivative means value derived from another asset.

It does not mean mathematical derivative.

## Main Types of Derivatives

## Forward Contracts

Agreement to buy or sell an asset in the future at a predetermined price.

## Futures

Standardized market version of forwards.

Usually traded on exchanges.

## Swaps

A sequence of forward-type contracts.

Often used to exchange:

- fixed payments
- floating payments

## Options

Derivatives with non-linear payoffs.

Basic options:

- calls
- puts

## Exotic Options

Any option that is not a simple call or put.

## Credit Derivatives

Derivatives whose payoff depends on whether debt defaults or not.

They played an important role in the 2008 Financial Crisis.

## Derivative Trading

## Exchange-Traded Derivatives

Characteristics:

- standardized contracts
- lower credit risk
- exchange guarantees payments

## OTC Derivatives

OTC means Over The Counter.

These are private contracts between institutions.

Characteristics:

- customizable
- less standardized
- higher counterparty risk

## Why Derivatives Exist

## Hedging and Risk Management

Derivatives help transfer risk between parties.

This is their main economic purpose.

## Complex Payoffs

Derivatives can create payoffs that are difficult to obtain using only basic securities.

## Speculation

Traders can use derivatives to bet on future price movements.

## Arbitrage

Derivatives can be used to exploit pricing inconsistencies.

## Flexibility

Derivatives allow financial structures that are not easily achievable with stocks and bonds alone.

## Important Quant Finance Ideas

## Linear vs Non-Linear Payoffs

Linear derivatives:

- forwards
- futures
- swaps

Non-linear derivatives:

- options

## Core Principle

Quantitative finance studies how to price derivatives using:

- underlying asset behavior
- risk
- arbitrage relationships
